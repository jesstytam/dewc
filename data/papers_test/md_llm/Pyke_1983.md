Aust. J. Bot., 1983, 31, 293-6

# Relationship between Time Since the Last Fire and Flowering in *Telopea speciosissima* R. Br. and *Lambertia formosa* Sm.

*Graham H. Pyke*

The Australian Museum, 6-8 College St, Sydney, N.S.W., 2000.

## Abstract

For *Telopea speciosissima* the percentage (or density) of plants in flower, the average number of flowers per inflorescence and the average number of fruits per inflorescence all appear to peak 2 years after a summer fire and decline thereafter. On the other hand, the average height of the inflorescences above the ground increases with time since the last fire. For *Lambertia formosa* the density of inflorescences appears to reach a peak 2 or 3 years after a summer fire.

## Introduction

The effects of fire on the Australian flora are many and are increasingly well studied (see Gill *et al.* 1981). There appears, however, to be little quantitative information on the relationships between time since the last fire and flowering of Australian plants (Gill 1981; Specht 1981). Only for *Xanthorrhoea australis* is there a well established relationship (Specht *et al.* 1958; Garnet 1971; Gill and Ingwersen 1976; Gill 1981): in some places this species flowers only rarely in the absence of fire (Specht *et al.* 1958; Garnet 1971).

The aim of this paper is to analyse spatial and temporal variation in flowering of *Telopea speciosissima* (waratah) and *Lambertia formosa* with respect to time since the last fire. This analysis is possible as a result of studies on the pollination biology of these plants and the foraging ecology of honeyeaters that visit them for nectar (Pyke 1980, 1981*a*, 1981*b*).

## Methods

The data presented were collected between December 1979 and December 1981 in the Brisbane Water National Park (lat. 33°32′22″S., long. 151°16′56″E.; elevation 170 m above sea level) and Ku-ring-gai Chase National Park (lat. 33°36′18″S., long. 151°16′16″E.; elevation 145 m above sea level). In each region, study sites are located in plateau-type, low open-forest (Benson and Fallding 1981) dominated by *Eucalyptus gummifera* (Gaertn.) Hochr., *E. haemastoma* Sm. and *E. oblonga* DC., with a shrub understory composed mainly of *Lambertia formosa* Sm. Both regions are located on plateau tops and hence all study sites have the same aspect. In both regions soils are deep, yellow and earthy (Benson and Fallding 1981) with no apparent differences between the study sites.

The Brisbane Water study site contains two areas in terms of fire history: one area last burnt in December 1976, the other in December 1979. The two areas are separated by a vehicle trail which stopped the more recent fire from spreading further. There are no apparent differences between the two areas in terms of soil or vegetation. The entire Ku-ring-gai Chase study site last burnt in 1965.

Flowering in *Telopea speciosissima* has been recorded in two ways. In the 1976 burn study area in Brisbane Water National Park, the total number of flowering plants was recorded for the spring flowering periods of 1979, 1980 and 1981. For each inflorescence
